## Overview
Peru is located in western South America, bordered by Ecuador and Colombia to the north, Brazil to the east, Bolivia to the southeast, Chile to the south, and the Pacific Ocean to the west and south.  
The country is megadiverse, featuring three main geographic regions: the arid coastal plains along the Pacific, the towering Andes mountains running north to southeast, and the tropical Amazon rainforest in the east.  
With a population of over 34 million, its capital and largest city is Lima, and it covers an area of 1,285,216 km², making it the third largest country in South America.

## History
- c. 12,500 BCE: Earliest known human settlement at Huaca Prieta.
- 3000–1800 BCE: Caral-Supe civilization flourishes, earliest known complex society in Peru.
- 1500–300 BCE: Chavín culture develops, centered at Chavín de Huantar as a religious phenomenon.
- 1000 BCE–200 CE: Paracas and Nazca cultures thrive on the southern coast, known for textiles and geoglyphs.
- 15th century CE: Inca Empire rises, becoming the largest pre-Columbian state in the Americas with capital at Cusco.
- 1532–1535: Spanish conquest begins; Lima founded in 1535 as capital of the Viceroyalty of Peru.
- 1551: National University of San Marcos established in Lima, the oldest continuously operating university in the Americas.
- 1821: Peru declares independence from Spain; independence secured after the Battle of Ayacucho in 1824.

## Geography & Climate
- Peru's geography includes three longitudinal regions: the arid coastal plain, the Andes mountain range, and the Amazon rainforest.
- The Andes mountains run from north to southeast across Peru, containing snow-capped peaks and high plateaus.
- The Amazon rainforest covers eastern Peru, encompassing the headwaters of the Amazon River and immense biodiversity.
- The coastal region is characterized by arid plains with minimal rainfall.
- Peru's climate varies from tropical in the Amazon basin, to desert along the coast, and temperate to cold in the highlands.

## Economy
- Main economic activities include mining, manufacturing, agriculture, and fishing.
- Growing sectors include telecommunications and biotechnology.
- Peru is part of The Pacific Pumas, a grouping of Latin American Pacific coast countries with positive growth and stable macroeconomics.
- Peru is an active member of the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation (APEC), the Pacific Alliance, the Comprehensive and Progressive Agreement for Trans-Pacific Partnership (CPTPP), and the World Trade Organization (WTO).
- A period of economic stability in the mid-19th century was driven by the exploitation of guano, ending with the War of the Pacific (1879–1884).

## Culture
- Peru's population includes Mestizos, Amerindians, White people, Afro-descent people, and Asian people, contributing to its cultural diversity.
- The main spoken language is Spanish, although many Peruvians also speak Quechuan languages, Aymara, or other Indigenous languages.
- This mixture of cultural traditions has resulted in a wide diversity of expressions in art, cuisine, literature, and music.
- Peru has gained international recognition for its vibrant gastronomy, blending Indigenous, Spanish, African, and Asian influences.
- Lima is considered a global culinary capital, home to award-winning restaurants such as Central and Maido.

## Fun Facts
- Peru covers an area of 1,285,216 km², making it the 19th largest country in the world.
- The Caral-Supe civilization, dating to around 3000 BCE, is considered one of the earliest cradles of civilization.
- Lima hosts the National University of San Marcos, established in 1551, the oldest continuously operating university in the Americas.
